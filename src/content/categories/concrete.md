## Concrete Calculators for Every Project

Concrete is the foundation of nearly every construction project — from a backyard patio slab to a full basement foundation. Getting your quantities right before you pour saves money, prevents mid-job shortages, and eliminates costly over-ordering.

Our concrete calculators use the same formulas contractors rely on every day, with built-in waste factors and both imperial and metric unit support.

## Which Concrete Calculator Do You Need?

**Concrete Slab Calculator** — The most commonly used tool. Enter length, width, and thickness to get cubic yards, cubic feet, and cubic meters instantly. Works for patios, garage floors, pool decks, and any flat pour.

**Concrete Bags Calculator** — If you're mixing by hand or using a portable mixer, this tells you exactly how many 40, 50, 60, or 80 lb bags you need. Accounts for yield per bag so you don't underorder.

**Concrete Footing Calculator** — Footings are the most critical pours in any structure. This calculator handles rectangular strip footings and gives you volume for the entire run.

**Concrete Column Calculator** — Round columns (sonotubes) for decks, fences, and structural posts. Outputs cubic yards and equivalent bag count.

**Concrete Driveway Calculator** — Driveways need 4–6 inches of concrete. This tool accounts for standard driveway dimensions and outputs volume ready to pass to your ready-mix supplier.

**Concrete Block Calculator** — How many CMU blocks for a wall? Enter wall dimensions and block size to get a precise count with mortar joints included.

## How Much Does Concrete Cost?

Ready-mix concrete typically costs **$125–$175 per cubic yard** delivered, depending on your region and mix design. For small jobs under 1 cubic yard, bag concrete (Quikrete, Sakrete) is more economical — expect to pay **$6–$9 per 80 lb bag** which yields about 0.60 cu ft.

Always add 5–10% to your calculated volume for waste, spillage, and subgrade variation.

## Concrete Mix Ratios

Standard concrete mixes follow the **1:2:3 ratio** — 1 part cement, 2 parts sand, 3 parts aggregate by volume. For high-strength applications, a 1:1.5:3 mix is common.

Ready-mix suppliers offer standard **3,000 psi** (residential slabs), **3,500 psi** (driveways, commercial floors), and **4,000 psi** (structural/heavy load) mixes.
