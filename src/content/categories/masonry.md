## Masonry Calculators for Walls, Pavers, and Structures

Masonry work requires careful material estimation — too few blocks and you halt work waiting on delivery, too many and you're stuck with unmixed mortar and unused material. Our masonry calculators give you exact counts before the first block is laid.

## Masonry Calculator Tools

**Concrete Block Calculator** — How many 8×8×16 CMU blocks for a wall? Enter wall dimensions, block size, and the tool accounts for mortar joints and standard course heights.

**Mortar Calculator** — Volume of mortar mix needed for block walls, brick veneer, or stone work. Accounts for joint width and coverage per bag.

**Grout Calculator** — Tile and paver grout quantity by square footage and joint width. Works for sanded and unsanded grout.

**Retaining Wall Calculator** — Block count, batter angle, and drainage aggregate for segmental retaining walls. Accounts for wall height and block dimensions.

**Paver Base Calculator** — Gravel base and bedding sand for any paver installation. Handles patios, walkways, and driveways in square feet.

**Post Hole Calculator** — Concrete volume for post holes. Enter hole diameter, depth, and post count to get cubic feet and bag count.

## Standard Masonry Dimensions

| Block Type | Nominal Size | Actual Size |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Standard CMU | 8×8×16 | 7.625×7.625×15.625 |
| Half block | 8×8×8 | 7.625×7.625×7.625 |
| Solid block | 4×8×16 | 3.625×7.625×15.625 |
| Standard brick | 4×2.25×8 | 3.75×2.25×8 |

Mortar joints are typically 3/8" (0.375") for block and brick work.

## Mortar Mix Ratios

**Type S mortar** (1:0.5:4.5 — cement:lime:sand) is the most common for load-bearing walls and below-grade work. It offers a good balance of strength and workability.

**Type N mortar** (1:1:6) is used for non-load-bearing walls, chimneys, and above-grade exterior work where flexibility is more important than strength.

One 80 lb bag of mortar mix covers approximately 25–35 standard CMU blocks, depending on joint consistency.

## Retaining Wall Design Principles

Retaining walls under 4 feet typically don't require engineered design. For walls 4–6 feet, consult local codes. Most retaining wall block manufacturers specify a 1" setback (batter) per course for gravity walls.

Always include drainage aggregate (3/4" crushed stone) and perforated pipe behind retaining walls to relieve hydrostatic pressure.
