## Fence Calculators for Posts, Panels, and Concrete

Whether you're installing a privacy fence, split rail, or chain link, the math always starts the same way: how many posts, how far apart, and how much concrete? Get those numbers right before you call the lumber yard.

## Post Spacing Guidelines

| Fence Type | Typical Post Spacing |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Wood privacy (6 ft) | 6–8 ft on center |
| Wood rail fence | 8–10 ft on center |
| Chain link | 10 ft on center |
| Split rail | 8–10 ft on center |
| Vinyl panel | 6–8 ft on center |

Closer spacing increases material cost but improves rigidity. In high-wind zones, drop to 6 ft spacing for privacy fences.

## Post Hole Depth Standards

Posts should be buried **1/3 of their total length**, with a minimum of 2 feet in frost-free areas. In frost zones, extend to the local frost depth.

- **6-foot fence post:** Bury 2 feet (use 8-foot posts)
- **8-foot fence post:** Bury 2.5–3 feet (use 10 or 12-foot posts)

For standard wood fence posts in a tube form, a **10-inch diameter hole at 2.5 feet deep** uses approximately 1.25 cubic feet of concrete — about 2 bags of 80 lb mix per post.

## Panel vs. Board Fencing

**Panel fencing** uses pre-assembled 6×6 or 6×8 sections. It installs faster but offers less flexibility for uneven terrain.

**Board-on-board fencing** is built in place with individual pickets. It's adjustable for slopes, allows airflow, and looks more finished. Material cost is slightly higher.

## Gate Planning

Every gate needs two dedicated posts set at least 6 inches deeper than standard posts and concreted in a larger diameter hole (12 inches minimum). Double-post gate systems use 4 posts for double gates. Don't try to hang a gate from standard fence posts.
