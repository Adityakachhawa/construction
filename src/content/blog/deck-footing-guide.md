Deck footings are the most safety-critical part of any deck project. They must carry the deck load to stable soil below the frost line. Under-sized or under-depth footings cause deck settlement, tilting, and structural failure. This guide covers how to get them right.

## How Many Footings Do You Need?

The number of footings depends on your post layout, which is driven by beam spans. A typical pattern:

- Posts every 8–10 feet along the beam run
- Beams spanning 8–12 feet between posts
- One footing per post

**Example:** A 12×20 ft deck with two beam lines, posts at 8 ft:
- Beam 1 (20 ft): 3 posts = 3 footings
- Beam 2 (20 ft): 3 posts = 3 footings
- **Total: 6 footings**

For attached decks, the ledger board at the house eliminates one beam line entirely — reducing footing count.

## Footing Size Requirements

Use our [Deck Footing Calculator](/calculators/deck-footing-calculator/) or use these IRC-based guidelines for 1,500 psf soil bearing (conservative):

| Tributary Load Area | Footing Diameter |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| Up to 25 sq ft | 10" diameter |
| Up to 50 sq ft | 14" diameter |
| Up to 75 sq ft | 18" diameter |
| Up to 100 sq ft | 20" diameter |

**Tributary area** = the deck area supported by each post. For a simple deck with equal post spacing, divide total area by number of posts.

## Footing Depth

> **Warning — Frost Heave:** A footing that doesn't reach below the frost line will heave upward every winter as the ground freezes and expand. Even a few inches of frost heave will rack your deck frame, crack ledger connections, and can pull lag bolts out of the house rim. This is non-negotiable — go to the local frost depth or deeper, every time.

Always extend below the frost line. Minimum depths by region:

| Region | Frost Depth |
|--------|------------|
| South (FL, TX, AZ) | 0–12 inches |
| Mid-South | 12–18 inches |
| Mid-Atlantic | 24–36 inches |
| Midwest | 36–48 inches |
| Northern states | 42–60 inches |

Verify with your local building department — they have exact required depths for your jurisdiction.

## Concrete Volume Per Footing

For a round sonotube footing:
**Volume = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × depth**

| Diameter | 36" Deep | 42" Deep | 48" Deep |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| 10" | 1.63 ft³ (3 bags 80lb) | 1.91 ft³ (4 bags) | 2.18 ft³ (4 bags) |
| 12" | 2.36 ft³ (4 bags) | 2.75 ft³ (5 bags) | 3.14 ft³ (6 bags) |
| 14" | 3.21 ft³ (6 bags) | 3.75 ft³ (7 bags) | 4.28 ft³ (7 bags) |

Bags assume 0.60 cu ft yield per 80 lb bag. Use our [Concrete Bags Calculator](/calculators/concrete-bags-calculator/) for totals across all footings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a permit for deck footings?**
Most jurisdictions require a permit for any attached deck or any deck over 30 inches above grade. The footing inspection is typically the first inspection — the inspector must see the footing holes before you pour concrete.

**Can I use deck blocks instead of poured footings?**
For floating decks (not attached to the house, close to grade), deck blocks may be acceptable and avoid permit requirements in many jurisdictions. Check your local code — requirements vary significantly.

**How long after pouring before I can put posts on the footings?**
Standard concrete reaches adequate strength for post installation in 24–48 hours for an unloaded post. Wait the full 7 days before applying significant beam and deck loads.
