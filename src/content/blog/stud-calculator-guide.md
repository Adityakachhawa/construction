Accurate stud counts are the starting point for every framing lumber order. Whether you're framing a single partition wall or a full addition, the formula is straightforward once you understand what goes into it.

> **Quick Answer:** Field studs = (Wall length ÷ spacing) + 1. Add 2 studs per exterior corner, 2 jack studs per door, 2 jack studs per window, and triple plate lumber (wall length × 3). Add 10–15% waste. Standard spacing is 16" OC for most residential work.

## The Basic Formula

**(Wall length in inches ÷ Stud spacing in inches) + 1**

For a 12-foot wall at 16" OC: (144 ÷ 16) + 1 = 9 + 1 = **10 studs**

This gives you the field studs — you still need to add for plates, corners, and openings. Use our [Stud Calculator](/calculators/stud-calculator/) for the complete count.

## Stud Spacing Options

| Spacing | Use Case | Stud Count vs 16" OC |
|---------|---------|---------------------|
| 12" OC | Heavy loads, tile walls | +33% more studs |
| 16" OC | Standard framing (most residential) | Baseline |
| 24" OC | Non-load-bearing, advanced framing | −33% fewer studs |

16" OC is the most common residential framing spacing. It works for both load-bearing and non-load-bearing walls and is compatible with standard 4×8 sheet goods.

## What to Add Beyond Field Studs

### Top and Bottom Plates
Each wall needs a **double top plate** and a **single bottom plate** (or double in some applications). Calculate plate linear footage = wall length × 3 (for triple plate).

### Corner Studs
Each exterior corner and interior corner (T-intersections) requires extra studs for nailing surface:
- **Exterior corners:** add 2 extra studs per corner
- **T-intersections:** add 1–2 extra studs per intersection

### Door Rough Openings
Each door opening requires:
- 2 full-height king studs (already counted)
- 2 trimmer (jack) studs: add 2 per door
- 1 header: add lumber for header span

### Window Rough Openings
Each window requires:
- 2 king studs (already counted)
- 2 jack studs: add 2 per window
- Cripple studs above and below: depends on window height

### Waste Allowance
Add 10–15% for mis-cuts, damaged material, and extras.

## Quick Reference: Studs Per Linear Foot

| Spacing | Studs per Linear Foot of Wall |
|---------|------------------------------|
| 12" OC | 1.0 studs/LF |
| 16" OC | 0.75 studs/LF |
| 24" OC | 0.5 studs/LF |

For quick estimates: a 16" OC framed house with 200 linear feet of exterior wall needs approximately 200 × 0.75 = 150 studs (field only, before extras for corners and openings).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What length studs should I buy?**
For 8-foot ceilings, buy **92-5/8" precut studs** — they're cut to the right length for 8-foot walls with standard plates. For 9-foot ceilings, use 104-5/8" precut studs. Custom heights require measuring and cutting standard 8-foot or 10-foot lumber.

**2×4 or 2×6 framing?**
2×4 is standard for interior partitions and most exterior walls in mild climates. 2×6 is used for exterior walls in cold climates to allow R-19 or R-21 insulation in the deeper cavity. Check your local energy code.

**How much do studs cost?**
Standard 2×4×8 studs currently run $3.50–$6.00 each at big-box stores, depending on market conditions. Steel studs for non-load-bearing partitions run $2.50–$4.00 each and resist moisture and fire.
