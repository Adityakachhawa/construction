Roof pitch is one of the most fundamental measurements in residential construction. It determines rafter length, material requirements, drainage performance, and what roofing products you can use. If you're building, repairing, or replacing a roof, you need to understand pitch.

> **Quick Answer:** Pitch = rise ÷ run, expressed as X:12. Measure from the attic with a level: hold level against rafter, measure 12" along it, measure vertical rise at that point — that number is your pitch. Most US homes are 4:12 to 6:12. ## What Is Roof Pitch?

Roof pitch expresses the slope of a roof as a ratio of **rise** (vertical height) to **run** (horizontal distance). It's written as **X:12**, meaning "X inches of rise for every 12 inches of horizontal run."

A **4:12 pitch** rises 4 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal distance. A **12:12 pitch** rises 12 inches for every foot — a 45° slope.

## How to Measure Roof Pitch

### Method 1: From the Attic (Most Accurate)
1. Find a rafter or the underside of the roof sheathing
2. Hold a level horizontally against the rafter, with one end touching the wood
3. Measure 12 inches along the level (the "run")
4. At the 12-inch mark, measure vertically up (or down) to the rafter
5. That vertical measurement is your pitch number

### Method 2: From a Ladder (Safe Alternative)
1. Position a ladder at the roof edge
2. Hold a level horizontally on the roof surface
3. Measure 12 inches horizontally from the wall end
4. Measure the vertical rise at that point
5. Rise in inches = pitch number

### Method 3: Speed Square
A speed square (rafter square) has a built-in pitch scale on the diagonal edge. Hold it against the rafter with the pivot point at the top, read the pitch directly from the scale.

## Common Roof Pitches and Their Applications

| Pitch | Slope | Common Use |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| 1:12 – 2:12 | Low slope | Commercial, shed dormers |
| 3:12 – 4:12 | Standard low | Ranches, shed roofs |
| 4:12 – 6:12 | Standard | Most residential homes |
| 7:12 – 9:12 | Steep | Cape Cods, dormers |
| 10:12 – 12:12 | Very steep | Steep gables, Victorian |
| Over 12:12 | Extreme | Tower roofs, special architecture |

## Minimum Pitch Requirements by Material

| Roofing Material | Minimum Pitch |
|-----------------|---------------|
| Asphalt shingles (3-tab) | 2:12 with underlayment |
| Asphalt shingles (standard) | 4:12 |
| Metal standing seam | 0.5:12 |
| Metal corrugated | 3:12 |
| Clay/concrete tile | 4:12 |
| Wood shake | 4:12 |
| Flat membrane (EPDM, TPO) | 0.25:12 |

Installing roofing below its minimum pitch creates pooling and leaks. Always check the manufacturer's spec before ordering material.

## Converting Pitch to Degrees

Some engineering calculations require slope in degrees rather than the X:12 format. The conversion:

**Degrees = arctan(pitch / 12)**

Common conversions:
- 4:12 = 18.4°
- 6:12 = 26.6°
- 8:12 = 33.7°
- 12:12 = 45.0°

## The Slope Multiplier: Key to Accurate Roofing Estimates

When estimating roofing material, you can't use floor plan area — you need actual roof surface area. The surface is always larger than the plan footprint because of the slope.

**Surface Area = Plan Area × Slope Multiplier**

The slope multiplier (also called the roof pitch factor) is:

**Multiplier = √(1 + (pitch/12)²)**

| Pitch | Multiplier |
|-------|-----------|
| 4:12 | 1.054 |
| 6:12 | 1.118 |
| 8:12 | 1.202 |
| 10:12 | 1.302 |
| 12:12 | 1.414 |

For a 40×30 ft house with a 6:12 roof:
- Plan area (one side): 40 × 15 = 600 sq ft
- Surface area (one side): 600 × 1.118 = 670.8 sq ft
- Both sides + waste: 670.8 × 2 × 1.10 = **1,475 sq ft** (about 14.75 squares)

## Rafter Length Calculation

The rafter is the hypotenuse of a right triangle formed by the run and rise.

**Rafter Length = √(Run² + Rise²)**

Or using the slope multiplier shortcut:

**Rafter Length = Run × Slope Multiplier**

For a 12-foot run at 6:12 pitch:
- Rise: 12 × (6/12) = 6 feet
- Rafter: √(12² + 6²) = √(144 + 36) = √180 = **13.4 feet**

Add the overhang (typically 1–2 feet) and the ridge board offset (half the ridge board thickness) for the total rafter length to cut.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the most common residential roof pitch?**
4:12 to 6:12 is the most common range for residential construction in the US. 6:12 is particularly popular — steep enough to shed water and snow effectively, not so steep that it's dangerous to walk on.

**Does roof pitch affect heating and cooling costs?**
Yes. Steeper pitches create larger attic volumes, which can be both an advantage (more insulation space) and a disadvantage (more air to condition if the attic isn't sealed). The relationship is complex and depends heavily on insulation approach.

**What is a "flat" roof?**
True flat roofs don't exist — all roofs must drain. "Flat" commercial roofs are typically 0.25:12 to 0.5:12. At those pitches, standard roofing products don't work; membrane systems (EPDM, TPO, modified bitumen) are required.

**Can I walk on my roof safely?**
At 6:12 and below, most people can walk on roofing with caution. Above 8:12, safety harnesses and roof jacks become important. Above 12:12, professional equipment is essentially required. Always prioritize safety over the cost of equipment.
