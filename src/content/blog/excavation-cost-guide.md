Excavation is often one of the first and most underestimated costs in a construction project. Whether you're digging a foundation, grading a yard, or cutting a trench, understanding the volume involved — and what that volume will cost to remove — is essential before you start.

> **Quick Answer:** Volume = Length × Width × Depth (in feet) ÷ 27 = cubic yards. Multiply by 1.2–1.4 for actual truck loads (swell factor). Contractor rates run $40–$150/hour; mini excavator rental is $250–$450/day for DIY.

## Excavation Cost Overview

| Project Type | Typical Cost |
|-------------|-------------|
| Hand digging (DIY) | Labor only |
| Mini excavator rental | $250–$450/day |
| Full-size excavator rental | $450–$800/day |
| Contractor excavation | $40–$150/hour |
| Basement excavation (full) | $3,000–$15,000 |
| Foundation trench (100 LF) | $1,000–$3,500 |
| Grading (1,000 sq ft) | $800–$2,500 |

## How to Calculate Excavation Volume

Volume = Length × Width × Depth (all in feet, ÷ 27 for cubic yards)

Use our [Excavation Calculator](/calculators/excavation-calculator/) for instant results.

**Example:** Foundation trench 60 feet long × 3 feet wide × 4 feet deep:
- 60 × 3 × 4 = 720 cubic feet
- 720 ÷ 27 = **26.7 cubic yards of spoil**

## The Swell Factor

Excavated soil occupies more volume than it did in the ground — this is called swell. A cubic yard of undisturbed soil becomes 1.2–1.4 cubic yards when loose.

| Soil Type | Swell Factor |
|-----------|-------------|
| Loose sand | 1.05–1.10 |
| Clay | 1.25–1.40 |
| Rock | 1.50–1.75 |
| Mixed soil | 1.15–1.25 |

Multiply your calculated volume by the swell factor to estimate actual truck loads needed for disposal.

## Equipment Selection Guide

**Mini excavator (1–4 tons):**
- Fits through standard gate or doorway
- Works well for trenching and landscaping
- Depth: typically 8–12 feet max
- Rental: $250–$450/day

**Compact track loader / Bobcat:**
- Best for moving and grading material on-site
- Not ideal for deep digging
- Rental: $300–$500/day

**Full-size excavator (20+ tons):**
- Foundation and basement work
- Depth: 20+ feet possible
- Rental: $600–$1,000/day (plus operator cost)

**Trencher (walk-behind or ride-on):**
- Ideal for utility lines, French drains
- Walk-behind: $150–$250/day rental
- Ride-on: $300–$500/day

## Soil Disposal

Excavated soil must go somewhere. Options:

1. **Spread on-site** — Cheapest. Works if you have grading needs elsewhere on the property.
2. **Haul to dump** — $75–$150/load depending on distance. Factor in your truck capacity.
3. **Give it away** — Post "free fill dirt" locally. Many homeowners with low spots will take it.
4. **Contractor disposal** — Included in most contractor bids; ask if it's separate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How deep can I dig by hand?**
Practically, hand digging beyond 3 feet becomes very difficult. Soil pressure makes it hard to remove material, and there's risk of cave-in. For anything deeper than 3 feet, rent equipment.

**Does excavation require permits?**
Most surface grading doesn't require a permit. Foundation excavation, utility trenches crossing property lines, and significant grade changes (more than a few feet) often require permits. Check with your local building department.

**What is "cut and fill"?**
Cut and fill is a grading technique where soil removed from high areas (cut) is used to fill in low areas on the same site. When cut equals fill, no import or export is needed — a significant cost saving. Our [Excavation Calculator](/calculators/excavation-calculator/) can help estimate cut vs. fill balance.
