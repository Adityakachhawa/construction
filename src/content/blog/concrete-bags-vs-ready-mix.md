The decision between bagged concrete and ready-mix concrete affects your cost, timeline, and the quality of the final pour. Get it wrong and you're either overpaying for delivery on a tiny job, or mixing 300 bags by hand on a project that should have been a truck.

> **Quick Rule:** Under 1 cubic yard → bags. Over 1 cubic yard → ready-mix. The crossover for most DIYers is around 1.5 yards once you factor in the short-load surcharge from a concrete plant.

Here's how to make the right call every time.

## The Simple Rule

**Under 1 cubic yard → bags. Over 1 cubic yard → ready-mix.**

This isn't absolute — there are jobs on either side of this line that break the rule — but it's a reliable starting point for 90% of projects.

## Bagged Concrete: When It Makes Sense

Bagged concrete (Quikrete, Sakrete, and other brands) comes in 40, 50, 60, and 80 lb bags. The 80 lb bag is most common at hardware stores and yields the best cost per cubic foot.

**80 lb bag yields:** 0.60 cubic feet
**60 lb bag yields:** 0.45 cubic feet  
**40 lb bag yields:** 0.30 cubic feet

Use our [Concrete Bags Calculator](/calculators/concrete-bags-calculator/) to find exactly how many bags you need.

### Advantages of Bagged Concrete

- **No minimum order.** Buy exactly what you need (plus extra).
- **No truck access required.** Great for backyard projects, tight spaces, or remote locations.
- **Work at your own pace.** Mix one bag at a time.
- **No scheduling pressure.** Ready-mix has to be placed within 90 minutes.
- **Widely available.** Home Depot, Lowe's, and most hardware stores carry Quikrete.

### Disadvantages of Bagged Concrete

- **Labor intensive.** Mixing 50+ bags is exhausting work.
- **More expensive per yard.** Ready-mix is significantly cheaper at scale.
- **Mix consistency varies.** Each bag is manually mixed — quality depends on the operator.
- **Physical limits.** An 80 lb bag is heavy to lift and work with.

### Best Applications for Bags
- Fence post setting (1–3 bags per post)
- Small footings and deck posts
- Repairs and patching
- Raised garden bed borders
- Anything under 20–25 bags total

## Ready-Mix Concrete: When It Makes Sense

Ready-mix concrete is batched at a plant and delivered by truck in precise amounts. You specify the volume (in cubic yards), mix design (psi rating), and slump.

### Advantages of Ready-Mix

- **Consistent quality.** Plant-batched concrete has precise water-cement ratios every time.
- **Lower cost at scale.** At 2+ yards, ready-mix is almost always cheaper per cubic foot.
- **Speed.** A truck delivers 8–10 yards in minutes. Mixing that by hand would take days.
- **Higher strength options.** You can specify 3,000, 3,500, or 4,000 psi mixes.
- **Professional results.** Even slump and mix design means better workability and finish.

### Disadvantages of Ready-Mix

- **Minimum order charges.** Most plants have a 5-yard minimum — small loads under that minimum get a "short load" surcharge of $50–$150.
- **Scheduling required.** You need to coordinate delivery, have your forms ready, and have enough crew.
- **Time pressure.** Concrete starts setting — you typically have 90 minutes from batching.
- **Truck access needed.** A concrete truck is large and heavy. Make sure it can reach your pour site.

### Best Applications for Ready-Mix
- Driveways (typically 8–15 yards)
- House foundations and slabs
- Garage floors
- Large patios
- Any pour over 1.5 cubic yards

## Cost Comparison

### Bags
At current prices (roughly $7–$9 per 80 lb bag, yielding 0.60 ft³):
- Cost per cubic foot: ~$13–$15
- Cost per cubic yard: ~$350–$405

### Ready-Mix
Typical prices in most US markets:
- $125–$175 per cubic yard delivered (3,000 psi standard mix)
- Plus short load fee if under minimum delivery quantity
- Some suppliers add fuel surcharges

**At 1 cubic yard:** Bags cost roughly $350–$400 in materials; ready-mix costs $125–$175 + delivery fee. Bags can be cheaper if there's a steep short-load fee.

**At 2 cubic yards:** Bags cost $700–$800; ready-mix costs $250–$350 + delivery. Ready-mix wins on cost.

**At 5+ cubic yards:** Ready-mix is always significantly cheaper.

## The Crossover Point

Factor in these variables to find your crossover:

1. **Ready-mix base price** in your market
2. **Short load fee** (if ordering under minimum)
3. **Your labor cost** (mixing bags isn't free)
4. **Project timeline pressure**

For most DIYers pricing their own labor at $0, the crossover is around 1.5–2 cubic yards. For contractors paying crew, it's often below 1 cubic yard.

## Special Case: Fast-Setting Concrete

Quikrete Fast-Setting Concrete is a specialized product for post installation. It can be poured dry into the hole and watered in — no mixing required. It sets in 20–40 minutes.

It costs more per bag than standard mix but eliminates mixing equipment and speeds up fence or deck post installation considerably. Not suitable for slabs or footings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I mix bagged concrete to match ready-mix quality?**
For small pours, yes. Modern pre-bagged concrete mixes use the same cement types as ready-mix. The main variable is water addition — too much water weakens concrete. Follow the bag instructions for water ratio.

**How many bags fit in a standard pickup truck?**
A half-ton pickup can carry about 40–50 bags of 80 lb concrete (3,200–4,000 lbs). A 3/4-ton truck can handle 60–70 bags. Don't exceed your truck's payload rating.

**What happens if my ready-mix truck shows up and I'm not ready?**
The driver will wait, but they're on the clock. Most trucks are paid in excess after a "free" window of 30–60 minutes. Get your forms, crew, and tools ready before the truck arrives.

**Is fast-setting concrete as strong as standard concrete?**
Fast-setting concrete typically reaches the same final strength as standard concrete (around 4,000 psi for Quikrete Fast-Setting). It just reaches usable strength much faster due to the accelerated chemistry.
