The driveway choice between asphalt and concrete is one of the most common home improvement decisions — and the right answer depends on your climate, budget, and long-term plans. Here's a clear breakdown.

> **Quick Pick:** Cold climate with heavy snow → asphalt (flexible, cheaper repairs, melts ice faster). Hot climate or long-term home → concrete (lasts 50+ years, stays rigid in heat). Tight upfront budget → asphalt. Curb appeal and resale → concrete.

## Cost Comparison

| Factor | Asphalt | Concrete |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Installation (per sq ft) | $3–$7 | $6–$12 |
| Typical driveway (600 sq ft) | $1,800–$4,200 | $3,600–$7,200 |
| Sealing (per sq ft) | $0.15–$0.25 | $0.25–$0.75 |
| Crack repair (per crack) | $100–$300 | $300–$600 |
| Full replacement | $1,500–$4,000 | $3,000–$7,000 |

Asphalt wins on upfront cost. Concrete costs more to install but typically requires less maintenance over its lifespan.

## Lifespan and Durability

| Material | Typical Lifespan | With Good Maintenance |
|----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Asphalt | 20–30 years | 30+ years |
| Concrete | 30–50 years | 50+ years |

Concrete consistently outlasts asphalt when properly installed. But asphalt is easier and cheaper to repair, so minor damage doesn't require full replacement.

## Climate Considerations

**Asphalt in cold climates:**
Asphalt is flexible at low temperatures, which makes it more resistant to freeze-thaw cracking. It also absorbs heat from sunlight, helping melt snow and ice faster.

**Concrete in cold climates:**
Concrete is susceptible to freeze-thaw damage, especially from de-icing salts (sodium chloride). Concrete driveways in cold climates should use calcium magnesium acetate (CMA) for ice control, not rock salt.

**Hot climates:**
Asphalt softens in extreme heat (above 120°F surface temperature) and can track or rut under heavy loads. Concrete stays rigid and is a better choice in very hot climates.

## Maintenance Requirements

**Asphalt:**
- Seal every 3–5 years
- Fill cracks as they appear
- Consider resurfacing after 15–20 years before full replacement

**Concrete:**
- Seal every 5–10 years (optional but extends life)
- Crack repair is more expensive
- Avoid de-icing salts

## Resale Value

Both materials add curb appeal. Concrete is generally considered more attractive by homebuyers and may add slightly more resale value, but the difference is minimal compared to condition — a maintained asphalt driveway beats a cracked concrete one.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose asphalt if:**
- You want lower upfront cost
- You're in a cold climate with heavy snow
- You want easy, inexpensive repairs

**Choose concrete if:**
- You want maximum lifespan
- You're in a hot climate
- You plan to stay in the home long-term
- Appearance is important to you

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you pour concrete over asphalt?**
Generally not recommended. Asphalt is flexible; concrete is rigid. Concrete poured over asphalt will crack as the asphalt moves. Remove the asphalt first, or use the asphalt as a base and add a concrete bond breaker.

**How thick should a concrete driveway be?**
4 inches for passenger vehicles; 5–6 inches for pickups and SUVs; 6 inches if you park heavy equipment or RVs. Use our [Concrete Driveway Calculator](/calculators/concrete-driveway-calculator/) to get exact volume.

**How long until I can drive on a new driveway?**
Asphalt: wait 24–48 hours for light vehicles, 5–7 days for heavy vehicles. Concrete: wait 7 days minimum for any vehicle traffic.
