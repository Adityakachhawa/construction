Drywall is sold by the sheet, but your walls are measured in square feet. Converting between them — while accounting for doors, windows, and waste — is where most drywall estimates go wrong. This guide walks through the process.

> **Quick Answer:** (Total wall + ceiling sq ft − door/window openings) ÷ 32 (for 4×8 sheets) × 1.10 = sheets to order. Standard thickness is 1/2" for walls and ceilings; 5/8" Type X required in garages and fire-rated assemblies.

## The Basic Calculation

1. Calculate total wall and ceiling area in square feet
2. Subtract for door and window openings
3. Divide by the sheet area
4. Add 10% for waste

**Standard sheet sizes:**
- 4×8 = 32 sq ft
- 4×10 = 40 sq ft
- 4×12 = 48 sq ft

4×8 sheets are the easiest to handle. 4×12 sheets reduce butt joints on tall walls but require two people to hang.

## Step-by-Step Example

**12×15 ft room, 9-foot ceilings, 2 doors, 2 windows:**

1. **Walls:** Perimeter = (12+15) × 2 = 54 LF × 9 ft = **486 sq ft**
2. **Ceiling:** 12 × 15 = **180 sq ft**
3. **Total raw area:** 666 sq ft
4. **Subtract openings:** 2 doors (20 sq ft each) + 2 windows (15 sq ft each) = 70 sq ft
5. **Net area:** 666 - 70 = **596 sq ft**
6. **Sheets (4×8):** 596 ÷ 32 = 18.6 → **19 sheets**
7. **With 10% waste:** 19 × 1.10 = **21 sheets**

## Drywall Thickness Guide

| Thickness | Application |
|-----------|------------|
| 1/4" | Curved walls, covering existing drywall |
| 3/8" | Rarely used; older repair applications |
| 1/2" | Standard walls and ceilings (most common) |
| 5/8" | Fire-rated assemblies, garages, bonus rooms above garage |

Most residential walls use **1/2" drywall**. Ceilings also typically use 1/2". Where fire rating is required (attached garage, between floors with rental unit), 5/8" Type X is mandatory.

## Special Drywall Types

| Type | Use | Notes |
|------|-----|-------|
| Standard (grey board) | Interior walls/ceilings | Most common |
| Moisture resistant (green board) | Bathrooms, humid areas | Not for direct water contact |
| Mold resistant (purple board) | Basements, kitchens | Better than green board |
| Type X (5/8") | Fire-rated assemblies | Required by code in many locations |
| Soundboard | Shared walls, theaters | Higher STC rating |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I install drywall on walls or ceilings first?**
Ceilings first — the wall sheets support the edges of the ceiling sheets, eliminating the need for additional ceiling clips along the edges.

**How much do drywall sheets weigh?**
A 4×8 sheet of 1/2" drywall weighs approximately 55 lbs. A 4×12 sheet is about 82 lbs. For ceiling installation, two people are strongly recommended.

**Can I use one sheet size for both walls and ceiling?**
Yes. Using 4×12 sheets on walls and ceiling reduces seam count but requires more handling effort. Many professional crews use 4×12 for ceilings and 4×8 for walls.

**What's included in a drywall package beyond sheets?**
For a complete installation you'll need: joint compound (mud), drywall tape, corner bead, drywall screws (1-5/8" for 1/2" drywall into wood studs), and potentially primer. Budget approximately $100–$200 in finishing supplies per room beyond the sheet cost.
