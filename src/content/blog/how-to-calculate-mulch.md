Mulch is one of the most cost-effective landscaping investments — it suppresses weeds, retains moisture, moderates soil temperature, and looks great. But ordering the right amount requires a quick calculation. Too little and you're making a second delivery trip; too much and you're moving wheelbarrows of mulch to the back corner of your yard.

> **Quick Answer:** Cubic yards = (Area in sq ft × Depth in inches) ÷ 324. Standard depth is 3 inches for beds, 3–4 inches for weed suppression. Under 3 cubic yards → buy bags. Over 3 cubic yards → bulk delivery is cheaper.

## The Calculation

**Volume (cubic yards) = (Area in sq ft × Depth in inches) ÷ 324**

The 324 constant converts square feet and inches directly to cubic yards without intermediate unit conversions.

**Example:** 400 sq ft of beds, 3 inches deep:
- (400 × 3) ÷ 324 = **3.7 cubic yards → order 4 yards**

## How Deep Should Mulch Be?

| Purpose | Recommended Depth |
|---------|------------------|
| Basic coverage | 2 inches |
| Standard beds | 3 inches |
| Weed suppression | 3–4 inches |
| Tree rings | 3–4 inches (keep away from trunk) |
| Pathways | 4–6 inches |

Never apply mulch more than 4 inches deep around plants — it can cause root suffocation and crown rot.

| Hardwood | Formal beds | Denser, slower decomposition |
| Cedar/cypress | Insect resistance | Premium price |
| Pine needles | Acid-loving plants | Free from your trees |
| Rubber mulch | Playgrounds | Does not decompose |

## Buying Mulch: Bags vs. Bulk

**Bags (2 cubic feet per bag):**
- About 14 bags per cubic yard
- Easy to handle, no delivery needed
- Cost: $4–$8 per bag → $55–$110 per cubic yard
- Best for: under 3 cubic yards

**Bulk delivery:**
- Most suppliers deliver 3-yard minimums
- Cost: $25–$45 per cubic yard + delivery fee
- Best for: 3+ cubic yards

The break-even point is usually around 3–4 cubic yards where bulk delivery becomes cheaper despite the delivery fee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How often should I refresh mulch?**
Organic mulch decomposes. Refresh every 1–2 years by adding 1–2 inches on top of the existing layer. Don't remove old mulch unless it's compacted or diseased — old decomposed mulch improves soil.

**Should I remove old mulch before adding new?**
Usually not necessary. If total depth exceeds 4 inches, remove some old mulch before adding fresh. If old mulch is matted or showing signs of disease, removal is a good idea.

**Is it cheaper to use gravel instead of mulch?**
Gravel costs more upfront but doesn't need replacement. Over 10 years, gravel is often cheaper. Mulch feeds the soil and looks more natural. It's a personal and functional preference. See our [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator/) to compare quantities.
